# MEP reporting automation - executive summary

**Decision requested:** prepare a controlled four-week user pilot, after data-access and confidentiality approval, to test whether reporting automation frees time for partner development. Broad deployment requires a separate decision.

**11 October 2026 - NIG case assignment.** Time savings, commercial figures and budgets are illustrative assumptions, not measured results or NIG accounts. All money below is USD.

## The opportunity

Ten Business Development managers support MEP, a platform distributing business applications through partners. They combine platform figures, meeting notes and delivery updates into reports. The ambition is **four to ten active partners in two years with the same BD team**, supported by additional engineering and platform operations where needed.

The chosen workflow is **weekly engineering-report preparation**. It scores 7.3/10 on Impact, Confidence and Ease, versus 6.0 for monthly partner reports, 5.7 for quarterly leadership reports and 5.3 for engineering prioritisation. Frequency and a clear output make it a useful first experiment. Scores require owner validation.

## Time released for partner development

The scenario reduces report production and checking from **eight to two hours per manager per reporting week**. Before: one hour calculating figures, four reading/grouping notes and drafting, two assembling the output, and one reviewing. After: fifteen minutes operating the workflow and one hour forty-five minutes reviewing and correcting.

Ten managers releasing six hours gives **60 hours/week, or 2,760 hours over 46 reporting weeks** for qualifying partners, following up proposals and coordinating onboarding. No salary savings are claimed.

Finding files, obtaining updates, choosing evidence and repairing incompatible spreadsheets remain manual. Extra preparation reduces the saving; shared report work must not be counted once per attendee. The demo proves workflow mechanics, not hours saved or successful partner acquisition.

## Revenue and profit assumptions

- **$2m annual revenue per partner**, recognised by NIG after contractual revenue shares, not total app sales. Four partners give $8m; ten give $20m. Real partners will differ.
- **20% profit before extra expansion costs**, including the existing operating cost base and current $3m MEP budget. Today: $8m x 20% = $1.6m. At target: $20m x 20% = $4m before the expansion allowance.
- **$500k additional annual platform cost**, detailed below, gives $4m - $0.5m = **$3.5m profit**, a 17.5% target margin. Deduct the increase once, not the whole platform budget again.
- **Simplified net-profit model:** the margin and extra costs represent assumed after-cost outcomes. Finance must reconcile actual operating costs, taxes and financing before this becomes a forecast. Revenue per partner 25% below the assumption would give $15m revenue and $2.5m target profit. Each further $100k of net cost reduces profit by $100k.

Neither the six-hour saving nor the commercial assumptions have been validated. No tested sales pipeline or conversion rate establishes the two-year goal.

## What works today

BD selects an audience, uploads files and downloads an HTML report or editable PowerPoint. The app calculates supported metric changes, uses Haiku to group and summarise notes, assembles output and saves reports for reopening. People check accuracy, confidentiality and relevance, then make decisions and follow up. Nothing is sent automatically.

The Overview dashboard is demonstration data, not updated by report uploads. Assisted intake, source connections, task creation and shared enterprise operation remain future work. Reuse suitable native tools and existing software; build the MEP-specific rules. Compare native alternatives before expansion.

## Staged platform access

**BD is the intended business user today; the local prototype has no login or enforced roles.** Engineering, vendor and partner are report audiences, not current user accounts. Start with an authenticated BD pilot, then internal engineers, then selected vendors and partners.

Engineers receive permitted technical work; vendors their service issues; partners their own commercial material and cleared reports. Protect other partners' data and internal-only findings. Permissions must cover source files, searches, reports, downloads and API actions. Identity, monitoring and access support are future work, budgeted only as rough allowances above.

## Risks, pilot and rollout

AI may omit or misinterpret information despite matching quotations. More files can mean more review. Limits are one spreadsheet, twelve documents and 40,000 combined text characters; there is no tested large-document or concurrent-user strategy. Synthetic benchmarks do not prove production reliability.

Notes are sent to the chosen AI provider; local history retains source material. Real use needs approved provider terms, source permissions, identity and retention/storage controls. Download review cannot undo unauthorised transmission. Check PowerPoint layouts in the recipient's viewer.

Allow 3-5 weeks of preparation, a two-week baseline and a four-week pilot with at least three users and twelve planned reports. Measure total effort including collection, repair, failures and review, plus cost per usable report. BD owns usefulness, engineering operation, and data/security owners approve processing.

Validate **permitted usable evidence, lower total effort at equal quality, and adoption that releases useful capacity**. Track qualified opportunities, agreements, launches and profit beyond the pilot. Pause for critical unsupported claims reaching a used report or unauthorised disclosure. Redesign below two hours median net saving per manager/week or below 75% usable planned reports; count failures. If successful, separately fund one approved source connection and one reviewed task destination before wider rollout.
